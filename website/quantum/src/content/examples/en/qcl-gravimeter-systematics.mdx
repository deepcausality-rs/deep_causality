The campaign finds the right one in all seven runs, one per systematic, for 166 s to 4,953 s of
instrument time. The instrument is the cold-atom gravimeter of Louchet-Chauvet et al., New J.
Phys. 13, 065025 (2011): T = 70 ms, and 22.1 µGal/√Hz in one configuration.

After the tide correction the gravimeter reads 5 µGal low. Seven systematics can each produce that
offset, and the example calibrates each candidate to produce it, so the passive reading fits all
seven. A response model says what each configuration of the instrument does to each systematic,
and QCL computes every predicted read-out from it. [The sensing page](/sensing/#plan) shows
which experiment moves which systematic.

## Three stages take seven candidates to one

1. **The baseline.** `baseline_with` observes the passive configuration first and refuses a
   candidate whose prediction the observation contradicts. At −5 µGal every candidate holds. At
   +5 µGal the baseline refuses tilt, which reads only low.
2. **The plan.** `design` prices each experiment as its setup time plus the integration time of
   its draws (`MinCostCover::timed`), and sizes the draws to the fewest that separate a pair by
   5 bits within the 5000 s white-noise range. It takes E1, E2, E3, E4, E7 and E8 for 4,956 s.
   Those six separate the wavefront from every rival, so the plan skips the 20,000 s temperature
   scan and identifies the wavefront by elimination.
3. **The campaign.** Each step runs the cheapest planned experiment that can separate a candidate
   still holding, judges every candidate within three standard errors, and stops at the first
   that holds and separates by 5 bits. After each step the experiments move to the new time; when
   the tide moves a planned prediction, the campaign plans again.
## A missing input either stops the run or misleads it

With no tide in the instrument context, every candidate contradicts the reading, and the baseline
refuses all seven. With clipping off the list, no pair needs the cloud displacement, so the plan
drops it, and the campaign blames Coriolis, which a turn flips as it flips clipping.

## The instrument is published; the setup times and the tide are placeholders

The instrument values come from Louchet-Chauvet et al. and Farah et al., on the same instrument;
the contrast, 40 %, from Ménoret et al.'s rubidium gravimeter. Each response
takes its coefficients from the paper it cites. The Zeeman shares come from Hu et al.'s field maps,
digitised by `deep_causality_quantum/papers/digitised/digitise_hu2017_fig6.py`. The light shift's
field-dependent third, 11 of 33 mrad, comes from Gauguet et al., and clipping's 14.2 µGal per mm
from Farah et al. The
setup times and the 80 µGal tide are placeholders, and `constants.rs` marks each one.

The predicted read-outs, the plans and the draw counts are computed. Every observation is drawn
from the cause under test, so each campaign's path and its cost follow from simulated data.
