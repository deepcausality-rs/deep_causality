The campaign and the full plan name the cause in all four candidate worlds. A leak from cloud A
into cloud B's detection signal takes one 60 s experiment to name, and its gradient bias is
−356 E for a 6 % leak. The instrument is the dual-cloud gradiometer of Sorrentino et al., Phys. Rev.
A 89, 023607 (2014): T = 160 ms, contrast 0.45, a 30 cm baseline.

A gradiometer runs two atom clouds off one laser and one mirror, and their read-outs move
together. If the shared phase alone moves them, differential extraction removes it. Fluorescence
leaking from one cloud into the other's detection signal biases the gradient, and so does platform
rotation. Each of the four candidates fits the passive read-out exactly, so passive data separates
none.

## What each stage decided

1. **The fit.** For each world the observations come from, the example fits the four candidates
   to that world's passive read-out through their fringe centres and differential phase.
2. **The screen.** `validate` confirms that each candidate is a normalised process, Markov, and
   free of a C₃.
3. **The plan.** `design_with` prices each experiment in instrument time and takes E1, E2 and E3
   for 721.8 s. The setup times dominate: E1 and E2 need 340 effective draws each, and the
   rotation step about 60,000 at 3 × 10⁻⁵ s a draw.
4. **The campaign.** It stops as soon as one candidate holds and separates: after E1 for a leak
   from A to B, after E2 for a leak from B to A, and after all three for the shared phase and for
   rotation.
5. **The consequence.** A flow reads the instrument's differential phase in the survivor's world,
   switches the context to the same world with the mechanism removed, and reads it again. The
   difference is the gradient bias: −0.044 rad, −356 E, for a 6 % leak, and +6.9 × 10⁻⁴ rad,
   +5.5 E, for rotation at Earth's horizontal rate.

A leak both ways is no candidate. The full plan reports it outside the model, because E2 shows a
leak the one-way candidate rules out; the campaign stops at that candidate after E1.

## What is typed, computed and sampled

The instrument values come from Sorrentino et al. The leak, the dark background and the setup
times are placeholders for a lab's measurements, and `constants.rs` marks each one. The fits, the predicted read-outs, the plan, the draw counts
and the biases are computed. Every observation is drawn from the world the run names, so each
campaign's verdict and cost follow from simulated data.
