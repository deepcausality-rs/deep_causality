The model is trivial for |u| > 2 and topological for |u| < 2, with Chern number +1 for 0 < u < 2
and −1 for −2 < u < 0. The Chern number is an integer that stays fixed however the material is
deformed, and it changes only when the gap closes.

The example computes it by two routes that share only the d-vector. One differentiates and never
forms a spinor. The other forms spinors and never differentiates.

## What you see

```text
     u    | C (quadrature) | C (Wilson loop) |  C  | phase
  --------+----------------+-----------------+-----+------------------------
     3.0  |      0.000000  |      -0.000000  | +0  | trivial (|u| > 2)
     1.0  |      1.000000  |       1.000000  | +1  | topological (0 < u < 2)
    -1.0  |     -1.000000  |      -1.000000  | -1  | topological (-2 < u < 0)

Agreement
  largest gap between the two routes      1.190e-15
  largest departure from an integer       1.190e-15
```

The second number is the one that matters. Nothing in either calculation rounds or snaps to an
integer. Both integrate a smooth function over a closed surface, and an integer comes out. The
first number makes the second believable, because the routes have nothing in common but the
d-vector.

## The two routes

| Route | How | What it needs |
|---|---|---|
| Quadrature | Berry curvature from exact ∂d/∂k, integrated by nested composite Simpson | derivatives, no spinors |
| Wilson loop | Berry flux around each plaquette of a k-grid, summed | spinors, no derivatives |

The derivatives come from the tangent functor, so no finite difference and no step size enters.

## Crates

This example does not import `deep_causality_quantum`. The quadrature comes from
`deep_causality_calculus` and the complex arithmetic from `deep_causality_num_complex`.
