The one-loop prediction is 80.369072 GeV, and the measurement is 80.377000 GeV. Fix the fine
structure constant, the Fermi constant and the Z mass, and the couplings, the remaining masses,
the decay widths and the resonance cross-section all follow.

At tree level `M_W = g·v/2` gives 78.909302 GeV, which misses by 1.459770 GeV. That gap is
roughly two percent, and the one-loop radiative corrections close it, dominated by the top quark
in the loop.

## What you see

```text
  M_W tree level        78.909302 GeV   from g*v/2
  M_W loop corrected    80.369072 GeV   from the loop solver
  the corrections move it by 1.459770 GeV
...
  M_W computed          80.369072 GeV
  M_W measured          80.377000 GeV   PDG
  deviation                 7.928 MeV   tolerance 20 MeV
  verdict            inside one-loop accuracy
```

## The tolerance is the claim

The run's tolerance is 20 MeV, the accuracy of a one-loop calculation, since the omitted two-loop
terms enter at about that size. The summary claims one-loop accuracy and the check tests exactly
that, so the two cannot drift apart. A looser tolerance would pass a calculation that had stopped
agreeing with the measurement.

Four stages compose as one flow: unification, symmetry breaking, gauge mixing and the Z
resonance. A stage that fails stops the ones after it and carries its error forward. The
invisible width of the Z is a prediction and not an input, and measuring it at LEP established
that there are three light neutrino generations.
