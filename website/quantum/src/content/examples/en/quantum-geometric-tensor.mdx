The example computes the quantum geometric tensor of a two-band model whose lower band is flat, and
feeds its metric into the Drude weight. Band curvature contributes 0.000000 meV·nm² to that weight,
and the quantum metric alone gives 0.037778 meV·nm². Conventional band theory says a flat band
cannot conduct, because it has no dispersion; the geometric term is what remains.

The tensor `Q_ij` gives one complex number per pair of axes. Its real part is the quantum metric
`g_ij`, which measures how far apart neighbouring states are. The Berry curvature is
`Ω_ij = −2 Im(Q_ij)`, a magnetic field in momentum space.

## What you see

```text
Q_ij for the flat band, and its decomposition
  pair      Re(Q_ij)      Im(Q_ij)      g_ij          Omega_ij
  Q_xx       0.004198      0.000000      0.004198      0.000000
  Q_xy       0.000000     -0.004198      0.000000      0.008395
  Q_yx       0.000000      0.004198      0.000000     -0.008395
  Q_yy       0.004198      0.000000      0.004198      0.000000

The symmetry the decomposition forces
  g_xy - g_yx                 0.000e0   the metric is symmetric
  Omega_xy + Omega_yx         0.000e0   the curvature is antisymmetric
  Omega_xx                    0.000e0   so the diagonal of it vanishes

  D = (D_conv + g~_xx * E_gap) * a^2
    flat band alone             0.000000 meV*nm^2
    with the geometry           0.037778 meV*nm^2
```

The metric is diagonal (`g_xx = g_yy = 0.004198` nm²) and the curvature is purely off-diagonal
(`Ω_xy = 0.008395`), because the two velocity operators have a relative phase. Equal phases would
leave `Q` real and the curvature zero. The three symmetry lines read zero, as they must for a
Hermitian tensor: the metric is symmetric, the curvature is antisymmetric, and the diagonal of the
curvature vanishes. The value `g_xx = 0.34 / 9²` follows from the one non-zero matrix element,
`⟨0|v_x|1⟩ = 0.5 + 0.3i`, and the 9 meV gap between the bands. The last two lines evaluate
`D = (D_conv + g~_xx · E_gap) · a²` twice: with the metric set to zero, and with the computed one.

## The step that is easy to skip

`Q_ij` comes out in nm², because the velocity matrix elements carry energy times length. The
transport kernel expects the metric divided by the cell area, `g̃_xx = g_xx / a²`, and
`reduced_metric` performs that division: 0.004198 nm² over (0.246 nm)² gives 0.069362. Passing
`g_xx` directly would hand the kernel an area where it expects a ratio, and since the kernel
multiplies the weight by a² again, the answer would be off by the cell area twice over.

## What this run leaves out

The model has two bands at a single point of the Brillouin zone, with the eigenstates equal to the
basis and the velocity matrix elements written down, not differentiated from a Hamiltonian. The
flat band sits at 1 meV, the remote band at 10 meV, and the cell length is 0.246 nm, the graphene
lattice constant. The Drude weight of 0.037778 meV·nm² therefore belongs to this toy model and is
no prediction for magic-angle twisted bilayer graphene. The run integrates nothing over the
Brillouin zone.

kernel come from `deep_causality_physics`, and `CausalTensor` from `deep_causality_tensor`.
