A direct cause: the run finds H1, qubit 1 driving qubit 2, after one experiment and in about 4 ms
on an Apple M3 Max. It runs `validate` and then `control` on one problem, so it exercises the hand-off between the two
halves. [The circuit version](/examples/qcl-crosstalk-circuits/) repeats the decision with circuit
candidates.

Two qubits show correlated errors. Three causal structures fit the passive data exactly, because
they are Markov equivalent, and a fourth is a cycle. The program refuses the cycle, screens the
other three, computes what each experiment predicts under each candidate, plans the cheapest
experiments that separate them, and runs the plan until one candidate survives.

## Four stages take four structures down to one

1. **`build()` refuses the cycle.** Under Definition 3.1 of van der Lugt and Lorenz the C₃
   criterion does not reject a cycle, so the builder does, and the error states the scope limit.
2. **`validate` screens the other three.** The normalisation check confirms that every factor is
   a conditional table that sums to one. The Markov check admits each candidate, because the
   factors are diagonal. The decomposability check runs on the structure each candidate's own
   supports encode. All three are admitted, and the screen is what `control` accepts.
3. **`design_with` computes the predictions and returns a plan.** A response model says what each
   experiment does in each candidate's world: holding a qubit excited replaces its table, and the
   echo replaces a driven qubit's table by its own excitation. Every read-out in the table follows
   from the candidates' tables. Holding qubit 1 excited and holding qubit 2 excited cost 1 each
   and cover all three pairs at the 5-bit floor.
4. **The campaign decides.** It runs the cheapest planned experiment, judges each prediction
   against the observation, and folds the verdicts. Each prediction is a read-out against a
   real-valued specification, so no commutation test runs. H1 separates from both rivals after E1,
   and the campaign stops there.

The plan holds two experiments because E1 alone cannot separate H2 from H3. E1 predicts 0.10
under both, so had the truth been H2 or H3, a read-out near 0.10 would leave both standing, and the
campaign would run E2 next: it predicts 0.40 under H2 and 0.10 under H3.

## The decision is computed; the tables, costs and shots are inputs

The plan, the predictions and the decision are computed. The candidates' conditional tables, the
experiments' costs and the response of each candidate to each experiment are inputs written into
`constants.rs` and `model.rs`, and the observation is sampled. Each qubit is excited with
probability 0.10 in every candidate, so passive data cannot separate them; a driven qubit is
excited with probability 0.40 when its driver is. The example draws its 1,024 shots from the Born
sampler at H1's own predicted read-out and then checks that the campaign finds H1. The run shows
the decision procedure reaching the right answer on stated assumptions. It reports no measurement
on a device.

The cost unit is arbitrary, so the totals of 2 and 5 come from the costs the example chose. The
factors are diagonal, so the Markov check has nothing to reject and the interventions carry the
whole discrimination.

## Sarovar et al. applied causal inference to crosstalk in 2020

Sarovar, Proctor, Rudinger, Young, Nielsen and Blume-Kohout (Quantum 4, 321, 2020) applied
statistical causal inference to crosstalk, adapting the PC algorithm on 2- and 6-qubit
processors. Their protocol runs a fixed set of experiments. This example declares the candidate
structures, screens them before any shots are spent, and chooses experiments against a cost.
