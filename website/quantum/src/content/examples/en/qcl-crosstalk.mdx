The run names H1, a direct cause from qubit 1 to qubit 2, and it takes 2 to 3 ms on an Apple M3 Max. It runs
`validate` and then `control` on one problem, so it exercises the hand-off between the two halves.
[The circuit version](/examples/qcl-crosstalk-circuits/) repeats the decision with circuit candidates.

Two qubits show correlated errors. Three causal structures fit the passive data exactly, because
they are Markov equivalent, and a fourth is a cycle. The program refuses the cycle, screens the
other three, plans the cheapest experiments that separate them, and judges each candidate's
prediction against an observation.

The excerpt keeps the lines that carry a decision and marks each gap with an ellipsis. The
program exits nonzero if any check it makes on its own decision fails.

## What each stage decided

1. **`build()` refuses the cycle.** Under Definition 3.1 of van der Lugt and Lorenz the C₃
   criterion does not reject a cycle, so the builder does, and the error names the scope limit.
2. **`validate` screens the other three.** The Markov check admits each one, because the factors
   are diagonal. The decomposability check runs on the structure each candidate's own supports
   encode. All three are admitted, and the screen is what `control` accepts.
3. **`design` returns a plan.** Holding qubit 1 excited and holding qubit 2 excited cost 1 each
   and cover all three pairs at the 5-bit floor. Process tomography would cover them at 200.
4. **`adjudicate` folds the verdicts.** Each prediction is a read-out against a real-valued
   specification, so no commutation test runs. H1 survives.

## Read before you cite

The predictions and the plan are computed, and the observation is sampled. The example draws its
1,024 shots from the Born sampler at H1's own predicted read-out and then checks that the
adjudication names H1. The run shows the decision procedure reaching the right answer on stated
assumptions. It reports no measurement on a device.

The predicted read-outs are constants in `constants.rs`, and the cost unit is arbitrary. The
factors are diagonal, so the Markov check has nothing to reject and the interventions carry the
whole discrimination.

## Prior work

Sarovar, Proctor, Rudinger, Young, Nielsen and Blume-Kohout (Quantum 4, 321, 2020) applied
statistical causal inference to crosstalk, adapting the PC algorithm on 2- and 6-qubit
processors. Their protocol runs a fixed set of experiments. This example names the candidate
structures, screens them before any shots are spent, and chooses experiments against a cost.
