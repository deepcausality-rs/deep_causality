The horizon follows ln(1/ε)/λ with λ ≈ 0.906, so it grows linearly in correct digits:
    f32  (ε≈1.2e-7 ):  t ≈ 17.6
    f64  (ε≈2.2e-16):  t ≈ 39.8
    F106 (ε≈1.0e-32):  t ≈ 81.3

Each ~16 extra digits roughly doubles the trustworthy forecast time. Past its horizon a
turbulence prediction is numerical fiction, however fine the step: for a chaotic flow the
only way to forecast further ahead is more precision. Here Float106, reached by a type
parameter, is the lever that buys the extra lead time.
