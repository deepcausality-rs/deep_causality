CAN A MONAD EXPRESS CAUSATION?
The answer to the question, and the stronger thesis that follows from it.

Written 2026-11-06. Companion to rework-epp.txt, which holds the wider repair plan, and the related
work findings, which hold the full citations.


================================================================================
1. THE ANSWER
================================================================================

No paper found states that monads cannot define causation, so that claim is not established. The
literature does point to a real trap, and it is narrow: a monad can express causal models, but its
composite arrow cannot identify one.

This is a synthesis of what the papers say, not a quoted result, and the search was not exhaustive.


================================================================================
2. WHY A MONAD CAN EXPRESS CAUSAL MODELS
================================================================================

Fong notes that Stoch is the Kleisli category of the Giry monad, and Bayesian networks are interpreted
in Stoch. The Caus monad of Wang et al. handles interventions and counterfactuals directly.


================================================================================
3. THE THREE REAL LIMITS
================================================================================

3.1 The composite forgets the causal structure.
    The chains [id, not] and [not, id] compose to the same arrow but answer differently when the
    middle value is forced. Checked in Lean 4.32; source in section 7.
    The literature says the same from other angles:
      - Jacobs, Kissinger, Zanasi perform surgery "within the syntactic category of string diagrams".
      - Di Lavore, Román, Széles describe interventions as "syntactic substitution".
      - Wang et al. say causal programs "need to expose more of their intension than probabilistic
        ones".

3.2 Normalized conditioning breaks associativity.
    Di Lavore et al. show that normalization fails to form a distributive law, which forces composition
    to be non-associative, and they use this to express both observations and interventions. This
    touches rung 1 and rung 3. It does not touch deterministic chains.

3.3 A monad states nothing about mechanisms.
    Its laws constrain how a lawful monad's bind and pure sequence steps, and they encode no causal
    mechanism. Causal content comes from invariance under intervention, which is why the autonomy
    clause is needed. This is probably why nobody defined causation this way, but no paper found says
    so.


================================================================================
4. THE STRONGER THESIS
================================================================================

The trap applies if the monad is the definition. It does not apply if the monad is the composition law.

    The monad carries the process.
    The term carries the structure.
    Autonomy carries the causal content.

Stated precisely:

  - A causal model is the term, meaning the chain or graph of named steps, and not the value or arrow
    it composes to.
  - Two terms with the same composite can be different causal models.
  - The monad laws equate evaluations. They do not establish that two models are the same.

This answers the objection that the axiom admits every program. The form admits every program, and
that is acceptable, because the form is not the definition of causation.

================================================================================
6. WHAT THE THESIS STILL NEEDS
================================================================================

6.1 It is not new in spirit. Separating syntax from semantics is the established move of Jacobs et al.
    and of the Markov-category line. Present it as aligning the EPP with that line, and put the
    contribution in the executable process term and its channels.

6.2 Term equivalence is undefined, and a referee will ask about it. If a model is a term, then when are
    two terms the same model? Reassociating a chain or adding a pure step should not create a different
    model, while [id, not] and [not, id] are different. A candidate: two terms are equivalent when they
    agree under every intervention at corresponding named steps. Otsuka and Saigo, and Makhija, offer
    comparison points. This definition needs to be written down and proved.

6.3 Reconcile with associativity. Under the candidate above, the monad laws should preserve model
    identity when the named steps are preserved. Prove that as a theorem. It is the bridge between the
    monad and the term.

6.4 Answer limit 3.2 for the P(U) lift and rung 3. Determine whether conditioning can be a lawful bind
    step or must sit outside the monad laws.

6.5 Change the definition in rework-epp.txt section 1 to name the term as the causal model.

6.6 The Lean check in section 7 is small. It shows the composite loses the structure, and it does not
    prove the thesis.


================================================================================
7. LEAN SOURCE OF THE CHECK
================================================================================

Save the block below as Extensional.lean, then run: lean +leanprover/lean4:v4.32.0 Extensional.lean
Result: both theorems pass, and neither depends on any axioms.

/- Scratch: a composite Kleisli arrow does not determine the result of a mid-chain intervention.
   Same arrow after composition, different counterfactual. Identity monad suffices. -/
def chainA : List (Bool → Bool) := [id, not]
def chainB : List (Bool → Bool) := [not, id]
def run (c : List (Bool → Bool)) (x : Bool) : Bool := c.foldl (fun v f => f v) x
/-- Force the value after step 0, then run the remaining steps. -/
def runDo (c : List (Bool → Bool)) (forced x : Bool) : Bool :=
  (c.drop 1).foldl (fun v f => f v) (let _ := x; forced)
theorem same_composite : ∀ x, run chainA x = run chainB x := by decide
theorem different_intervention : runDo chainA true false ≠ runDo chainB true false := by decide
#print axioms same_composite
#print axioms different_intervention
