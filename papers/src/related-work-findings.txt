RELATED WORK FOR THE EFFECT PROPAGATION PROCESS
Precedent for causality via monads, and what it changes in papers/src/rework-epp.txt.

Search date: 2026-11-06.
Method: arXiv API queries over abstracts and titles, combining monad, monadic, Kleisli, Markov
category, string diagrams, effect handlers and proof-assistant terms with causal, causality,
intervention, counterfactual and do-calculus. Web searches followed, and the reference lists of the
closest papers were checked. Key papers were read in full text for their use of monads.
Not searched: ACM Digital Library and Google Scholar. Treat the list as strong, not exhaustive.
Full text was not read for every entry; section 7 lists what to confirm before citing.


================================================================================
1. FINDING
================================================================================

Monads have solid precedent as a semantics for causal models. No paper found uses a monad to define
causation. The citation gap closes, and the axiom m2 = m1 >>= f remains the paper's own claim, to be
defended on its merits with autonomy and dependence (rework-epp.txt, section 1).

Every monadic precedent found has a probability structure as its carrier. No precedent was found for
a carrier of state, error and audit log. This is an absence in a limited search, so word it that way.


================================================================================
2. DIRECT PRECEDENT: A MONAD CARRIES THE CAUSAL MODEL
================================================================================

Wang, Stein, Bingham, Feser, Kammar, Lee, Yallop. Typed Abstractions for Causal Probabilistic
Programming. Talk abstract, LAFI 2026 (POPL 2026 workshop). No DOI found.
https://www.cl.cam.ac.uk/~jdy22/papers/typed-abstractions-for-causal-probabilistic-programming.pdf
    Defines a Haskell monad Caus representing a causal model. Caus is a reader monad: a model is a
    suspended computation executed under intervention instructions. It is graded by the available
    intervention points. Counterfactual worlds use multi-values. Distills the core design of ChiRho.
    Use: the closest precedent, and a direct comparison for the context channel, since both supply
    the world through an environment-like channel.

Di Lavore, Román, Széles. Normalized Probabilistic Semantics is Not Associative. arXiv:2510.01131.
    Normalization fails to form a distributive law, which forces the composition of normalized
    stochastic kernels to be non-associative. Introduces the magmad, a non-associative monad, of
    normalized distributions. It expresses observations and interventions, and derives the front-door
    and back-door criteria.
    Use: must cite. See section 5.1.

Román. Causal Probabilistic Programming via Magmadic Do-Notation. arXiv:2609.02873.
    A do-notation metalanguage over the same magmad. Its intervene construct is rewritten through the
    ID algorithm. Addresses identifiability, which the EPP places out of scope.
    Use: related work on causal do-notation, and a contrast in scope.

Sargsyan. A cubical formalisation of conditional independence, Bayesian conditioning, and Pearl's
d-separation soundness. arXiv:2606.20351.
    Cubical Agda: a probability monad as a higher inductive type, also verified as a Markov category,
    with d-separation soundness on finite DAGs.

Sargsyan. A cubical formalisation of topos causal models: intervention, forcing, and a contextuality
obstruction. arXiv:2607.15629.
    Sequel in Cubical Agda. Shows from the monad laws why intervention and observation differ under
    confounding, which is the same point as Appendix A's error.
    Use for both: verified monadic precedent.

3. FOUNDATIONS: KLEISLI CATEGORIES AND PROBABILITY MONADS
================================================================================

Fong. Causal Theories: A Categorical Perspective on Bayesian Networks. arXiv:1301.6201.
    Causal theories as symmetric monoidal categories. Notes that Stoch is the Kleisli category of the
    Giry monad, and Rel that of the power set monad.

Jacobs, Széles, Stein. Compositional Inference for Bayesian Networks and Causality. arXiv:2512.00209.
    Inference and causality in the Kleisli categories of the distribution and subdistribution monads.

Mahadevan. Intuitionistic j-Do-Calculus in Topos Causal Models. arXiv:2510.17944.
Mahadevan. Topos Causal Models. arXiv:2508.08295.
    The Kleisli category of the distribution monad as the probabilistic setting for topos causal
    models. Counterfactuals are built inside the topos.

Ramsey, Pfeffer. Stochastic Lambda Calculus and Monads of Probability Distributions. POPL 2002.
https://www.cs.tufts.edu/~nr/pubs/pmonad-abstract.html
    Probability distributions form a monad, giving a stochastic lambda calculus its semantics.

Ścibior, Ghahramani, Gordon. Practical Probabilistic Programming with Monads. Haskell 2015.
DOI 10.1145/2804302.2804317.

Moggi. Notions of Computation and Monads. Information and Computation 93(1), 1991.
DOI 10.1016/0890-5401(91)90052-4.
    The journal version. The bibliography has Moggi 1989 and Wadler 1992; add this one.

Giry. A categorical approach to probability theory. 1982.
    Taken from the citation trail. Not opened; confirm the details.

  Lorenz, Tull. Causal models in string diagrams. arXiv:2304.07638.

Machine-checked causality:
  Makhija. Graph Surgery and the Do-Operator: A Precise Correspondence for Acyclic Structural Causal
  Models. arXiv:2608.17634. Lean 4 artifact: https://github.com/SatpreetMakhija/graph-surgery-do-operator
      Proves Graph(F_i) = Surg(Graph(F), T_i): replacing target mechanisms removes exactly the
      dependencies graph surgery removes, for deterministic acyclic SCMs with finitely many variables.
      Also covers sequential interventions and ancestor locality.
  Zhang, Luo, Bielicke, Jun, Chlipala. Causality and Semantic Separation. PLDI 2026. arXiv:2604.22041.
      Mechanizes in Rocq the match between d-separation and a semantic definition inspired by

5. WHAT THIS CHANGES IN rework-epp.txt
================================================================================

5.1 Associativity needs an answer (section 4.3 d). Di Lavore et al. show that normalization forces
    composition to be non-associative. The P(U) lift and rung 3 need conditioning. Determine whether
    normalized conditioning can be a lawful bind step in the associative causal monad, or whether it
    must sit in a stage outside the monad laws, for example as an unnormalized value with
    normalization at the boundary. Add this to the status table as open. A scholar in this area will
    ask.

5.2 The verified niche is not empty. openspec/notes/archive/causal-algebra/Formalization.md says no
    work in these lines is tied to a proof assistant. Makhija, Zhang et al. and Sargsyan refute this.
    Do not repeat the claim, and correct the note.

5.3 Graph do has a Lean precedent (sections 3.1 and 4.3 c). Read Makhija's artifact before starting.
    Cite it for the graph-to-mechanism correspondence, and make the contribution the correspondence
    with the executable graph evaluator.

5.4 Reposition section 6 of the core paper. Lead with the comparison against Caus and ChiRho, since
    those are the nearest systems: what the state, error and log channels and runtime mechanism change
    give beyond a reader monad over probability, and where they give only convenience.

5.5 Related work list (core paper section 10). Add sections 2 and 4 of this file to the names already
    there. Also answer "why not a magmad?" alongside "why not a Markov category?".

6. FALSE FRIENDS: DO NOT CITE AS CAUSAL PRECEDENT
================================================================================

Liu, Cheng, Hudak. Causal Commutative Arrows. JFP 2011.
Uustalu, Vene. Signals and Comonads. JUCS 2005.
    Here "causal" means an output depends only on past inputs. Useful only as arrow and comonad
    background.

Moss, Staton. A causal Markov category with Kolmogorov products. arXiv:2512.24417.
    "Causal" names an axiom of Markov categories.

Mahadevan. GAIA: arXiv:2402.18732. Universal Imitation Games: arXiv:2405.01540.
    "Causal monad" names a DAG transitive-closure monad. The EPP uses the same phrase for something
    else, so note the collision in the paper.

Lu. Causal-net category. arXiv:2201.08963.
    A Kleisli structure on acyclic graphs, not about causal inference.

7. CONFIRM BEFORE CITING
================================================================================

- Wang et al. is a talk abstract without a DOI. Check the citation form with the authors or LAFI.
- Icard: one listing dates the paper 2025, while the proceedings are from 2017.
- The 2026 preprints from Román, Makhija and Sargsyan may not be peer-reviewed yet. Check their status
  at submission time.
- Abstract-level only: Otsuka and Saigo, Yin and Zhang, Lorenz and Tull, Tavares et al., MultiVerse.
  These were searched for monad terms, not read in full.
- Compare this list against the papers the author has already found.

8. NOTE FOR THE TELEOLOGY PAPER
================================================================================

No monad precedent found bears on the teleology. The findings that do, from the code review:

- papers/src/EPP/epp_chapters/teleology.tex line 97 says conflict resolution produces a
  "conflict-free" set. The code removes a norm only along a defeat edge the author drew, and an
  Impermissible norm wins the verdict over an unconnected Obligatory one by a fixed rule.
- Lex Superior, Lex Specialis and Lex Posterior collapse into one lexicographic order, with priority
  first, so specificity alone never creates an exception.
- deep_causality_ethos/src/types/effect_ethos/deontic_inference.rs lines 108 and 115 fail open on an
  erroring uncertain predicate. See rework-epp.txt section 3.6.
- The Ethos has no Lean coverage, while chapter 4 says a system's behaviour "can be proven" to remain
  aligned with its principles. teleology.tex lines 134 to 140 correctly list this verification as
  open, so align the two.
- examples/csm_examples/csm_effect_ethos does not gate the CSM, and its one norm is always active.
