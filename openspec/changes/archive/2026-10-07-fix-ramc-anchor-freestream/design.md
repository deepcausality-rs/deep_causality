## Context

The proposal records the defect. The facts the design rests on, with their sources:

- **Flight datum.** Grantham (1970), NASA TN D-6062, p. 18: "The Ka-band sensor at station 1 did
  detect critical density at about 236 000 ft (71.93 km)". Station 1 sits at x/D = 0.15 (p. 6).
  Ka-band is 35 000 MHz (Table I). Critical density N_e,cr = 1.287e-8 f² (cm⁻³, f in Hz; p. 11),
  and the slope technique reads N_e,pk/N_e,cr = 0.63 (p. 11). Figure 31(a) plots that point at
  ≈ 1e13 cm⁻³. Below 71.93 km station 1 is overdense at Ka-band, the highest frequency flown. The
  primary data period ends at 185 000 ft (56.39 km), where the beryllium cap was ejected.
- **Flight freestream.** Parent, Thoguluva Rajendran & Omprakas (arXiv:2111.09432): 61 km: Mach
  23.9, 255.9 K, q 8 kPa; 71 km: Mach 25.9, 217.9 K, q 2.28 kPa. Rodriguez Fuentes & Parent, Phys.
  Fluids 37, 013609 (2025), repeat the Mach numbers. Both derive V ≈ 7.66 km/s, matching Grantham's
  Table VII (7.66 km/s at 62 and 71 km).
- **Atmosphere.** U.S. Standard Atmosphere 1976 (NASA-TM-X-74335, NTRS 19770009539): Table 2
  (k, R*, g₀, P₀, r₀, T₀), Table 4 (layer base geopotential heights and gradients). A generator from
  these constants reproduces Table I at 61, 71 and 72 km to five significant figures (61 km: 244.274
  K, 19.157 Pa, 2.7321e-4 kg m⁻³).

## Goals / Non-Goals

**Goals:**

- Every RAM-C comparison compares like with like: the same altitude, a cited freestream, and the
  flight datum's own meaning (a value at 71.93 km, a lower bound below it).
- The descent flies an undistorted standard atmosphere, interpolated without a 2× density error.
- Every changed number is regenerated by running the program that prints it.

**Non-Goals:**

- Recalibrating the chemistry. The finite-rate network, the Millikan–White closure and γ_eff = 1.1 stay
  as they are; the change corrects the condition they are evaluated at.
- Re-earning a passing verdict. If the corrected physics lands outside a band, the gate reports it.
- 6-DOF, winds, or a RAM-C-geometry vehicle. The corridor vehicle stays a labelled point-mass probe.

## Decisions

**D1. Anchor value and meaning.** N_e,pk = 0.63 × 1.287e-8 × (3.5e10)² cm⁻³ = 9.93e12 cm⁻³ =
9.93e18 m⁻³ at 71.93 km, from Grantham's own relation; below 71.93 km down to 56.39 km the same number
is a lower bound. Alternative considered: keep the round 1e19 "peak" figure. Rejected: it hides that
the reading is a single crossing and that lower altitudes are bounds.

**D2. Atmosphere source and resolution.** US-1976, generated from Tables 2 and 4 at 1 km spacing,
0–90 km (91 rows); 86–90 km extend isothermally at the 86 km temperature, hydrostatic in geometric
altitude (agrees with Table I to 0.2 % at 90 km). Alternative: transcribe Table I by eye. Rejected for
error risk; the generator is checked against transcribed spot rows instead. Alternative: keep the
calibrated pinning. Rejected: it distorts altitude, which is the defect.

**D3. Density interpolation.** `DescentSchedule::sample` interpolates `ln n_tot` linearly; T and a stay
linear. Exact for an isothermal layer and within the generator's accuracy at 1 km spacing. Alternative:
leave the sampler linear and rely on the dense table. Rejected: any coarse table a caller supplies would
keep the 2× error.

**D4. Stagnation-line condition.** The harness flies the cited 71 km condition: M = 25.9, T∞ =
217.9 K, q = 2.28 kPa; V = M·√(γRT∞), ρ∞ = 2q/V², n∞ = ρ∞/m̄. It compares against D1's anchor and
records the 0.93 km between the 71 km condition and the 71.93 km crossing as part of the comparison.
`MACH` stops being independent of velocity and temperature.

**D5. Corridor comparison station.** The corridor compares its `n_e` at the 71.93 km crossing (read
from its per-step trace) against D1. Its light probe (β ≈ 170 kg/m²) decelerates far more than RAM-C II
below the anchor, so its 61 km value is reported beside D1's lower bound with its speed, not gated; the
stagnation-line harness gates the lower bound at RAM-C II's own 61 km freestream. The
truth entry velocity is scaled, at fixed flight-path angle, so the vehicle crosses 71.93 km at
7.66 km/s; this replaces the old sizing for "the calibrated Mach-25 station at 61 km".

**D6. Band policy.** The ±0.70-decade allowance keeps its width; it is a chemistry-model-spread
allowance and states no source, so the gates that use it stay `[tripwire]`. The stagnation-line 61 km
lower bound uses the flight datum with no allowance and is `[reference]`. Other `[tripwire]` bounds that the corrected
physics moves (exit-altitude band, onset spread, cold-drift factor, Park-2T controller value, aim
offset sizing) are re-pinned from the corrected run, each with the measured value and the date.

**D7. Reference scales.** `T_REF`, `N_REF`, `U_REF` keep their values. They nondimensionalize the inflow
and re-dimensionalize the outputs symmetrically, and the seed, `S_REF` and `DT_SOLVER` are expressed in
their units; changing them would change conditioning, not physics. Their documentation states that they
are scales of the order of the anchor-station post-shock state.

**D8. Descent peak.** The corridor reports the descent's peak `n_e` and its altitude, and gates that the
peak lies inside the flow-resolved denied window (`[tripwire]`). No flight datum applies below 56.39 km.

**D9. Regeneration order.** Stagnation-line harness → corridor → weather (writes the table) →
retropropulsion (reads it) → the four other CFD examples and the Park-2T harness. Documentation follows
the regenerated artifacts.

## Risks / Trade-offs

- [The corrected prediction falls outside ±0.70 decades] → the harness and corridor report failure;
  baselines are committed as failing, per the verification convention; the documentation states the
  offset.
- [The denser mid-atmosphere decelerates the light probe higher, moving every corridor number] →
  expected; all quoted numbers are regenerated; website, tutorial and video text that quotes them is
  listed for update.
- [The retropropulsion descent no longer reaches its ignition corridor inside its horizons] →
  re-measure; adjust only labelled setup horizons, and report if the physics no longer supports the
  scenario.
- [Log-linear interpolation changes results for other callers] → the only in-tree caller is
  `CompressibleMarchRun::pre_step`; the sampler test is rewritten to the new contract.
